社会理论译丛 — a long-running series of annotated Chinese translations of social-theory texts that I think matter but that have no good Chinese edition. Each work is rendered as a self-contained **bilingual "critical edition"**: fluent prose, key terms glossed in the original language, translator's notes (校注) in the margin, figures lifted faithfully from the source, and in-page navigation — and each is published as its own page.

Everything here is produced with **[scholarly-translation](https://github.com/andrehuang/scholarly-translation-skill)**, an open-source [Claude skill](https://docs.claude.com/en/docs/agents-and-tools/agent-skills/overview) I wrote for exactly this kind of work — it fixes the workflow (translate chapter by chapter into one growing file), the terminology discipline (a glossary settled up front and reused everywhere), and the design system. The series is the skill's proving ground: each new translation both tests and sharpens the tool, and every page links back to it.

### The theme — 群体如何成为实体

The works I pick are not random. They circle one question:

> **How does an aggregate of individuals become an entity?** — how a mere collection of people hardens into a *thing* that can hold resources, outlive its members, and act in its own name.

The most demanding form of that question is the birth of a **corporate actor** (法人行动者): an entity that persists across turnover, owns things, and bargains *asymmetrically* with the very natural persons inside it. The four works below give four different answers — and disagree productively about whether such a birth is sudden or slow, willed or structural, legible to its participants or invisible to them.

### Reading &amp; translation plan

I read these as four answers to one question, running from the most *legible, intentional, instantaneous* birth (Searle's declarative moment) to the most *illegible, structural, slow* one (Padgett's quiet structural condensation). Coleman frames the whole field; the two Padgett works anchor the opposite, structural pole.

**John Searle — *The Construction of Social Reality* (1995) / *Making the Social World* (2010)** · <span style="color:#7a2b25;">计划中 Planned</span>
A group becomes an entity through a single constitutive **speech act**: someone declares "we hereby form X," and once the declaration wins **collective recognition** (uptake), it lights up a whole bundle of rights and obligations (**deontic powers**) — and the entity "counts." A *declarative, instantaneous, legible* birth: there is a clear linguistic moment when it happens.

**James Coleman — *Foundations of Social Theory* (1990)** · <span style="color:#7a2b25;">计划中 Planned</span>
Becoming an entity is the extreme form of the hardest move in social theory — the upward micro→macro arrow. Not individuals aggregating into a macro *statistic*, but crystallizing into a *new category of being* — a corporate actor that survives turnover, holds resources, and plays an asymmetric game against natural persons. Coleman is the one who fixes "becoming an entity" *as a problem, as a category* — but he marks the gap and then imports the legal person as an exogenous input.

**John Padgett &amp; Walter Powell — *The Emergence of Organizations and Markets* (2012)** · <span style="color:#7a2b25;">计划中 Planned</span>
Becoming an entity is the **genesis** of a new organizational form — it grows out of the overlap and spillover of kinship, economic, and political networks, gains new function by carrying a relational practice from one domain into another (**transposition**), and persists across turnover once the network becomes self-reproducing (**autocatalysis**). A *generative, longue-durée, cross-domain* path — concerned with where new actors come from, not which survive.

**John Padgett &amp; Christopher Ansell — *"Robust Action and the Rise of the Medici"* (AJS, 1993)** · <span style="color:#977028;font-weight:600;">已完成 Available ↓</span>
The birth of one concrete corporate actor. Here the corporate actor is a *product of structure, not intention*, and is often **illegible**: the Medici party slowly hardened, out of the intersection of several only-partly-overlapping networks, through multivocal, unreadable **robust action** — and at the time none of its members could have said it was a unified project. A *blurred, slow, illegible, structure-driven* birth — exactly the inverse of Searle's declarative path.

### Shared glossary (in progress)

One contribution of reading these together is to render the shared vocabulary *consistently* across four authors — so a term means the same thing on every page. This master glossary is provisional and grows with the series.

| English | 中文 | 简注 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| corporate actor | 法人行动者 | 能换人存续、持有资源、与自然人不对称博弈的实体 |
| robust action | 稳健行动 | 多义、不锁定单一目标、对多方都"说得通"的行动（Padgett &amp; Ansell） |
| multivocality | 多义性 | 同一行动在不同受众处可有不同读法 |
| ecological control | 生态控制 | 凭借结构位置、而非直接干预实现的控制（Padgett） |
| micro–macro transition | 微观—宏观转换 | 个体层面如何"上行"为宏观存在物（Coleman） |
| Declaration | 宣告 / 声明式言语行为 | 经由陈述本身使其成真的言语行为（Searle） |
| deontic powers | 道义权力 | 权利、义务、授权等规范性力量（Searle） |
| collective recognition / uptake | 集体承认 | 声明须被集体接受，方才"算数"（Searle） |
| transposition | 移植 / 换位 | 把某一领域的关系实践搬入另一领域（Padgett &amp; Powell） |
| autocatalysis | 自催化 | 网络自我再生产，穿过成员更替而存续（Padgett &amp; Powell） |
